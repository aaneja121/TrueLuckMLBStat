Pitcher Contact Luck

How many runs the outcomes of the batted balls a pitcher allowed added or removed, relative to what that contact itself was expected to produce, across the {{ pitchers.season }} season.

{# The provenance line. This route only ever exists in a local build, but nothing about it should depend on the reader knowing that. #}

Development prototype. {{ pitchers.season }} development-season data only. Not scored for any later season, not part of the published leaderboard, and not a projection.

What the number means

Every eligible batted ball has an expected run value, from the contact itself, and the run value of what actually happened. Cumulative Contact Luck Runs adds up the difference across the batted balls a pitcher actually allowed. A positive total means those outcomes went better for the pitcher than the contact predicted; a negative total means they went worse.

The total is exact for the batted balls listed, given the scoring model it is measured with. It is deliberately sensitive to workload: a pitcher who allowed more batted balls had more chances to accumulate a total in either direction, which is why the batted-ball count sits beside every number on this page. It describes what already happened. It is not a measure of pitching skill and not a forecast.

Per 100 normalizes the same total for opportunity, and is shown with the uncertainty that comes with it. Because it is spread across very unequal samples it is a secondary reading here, never the order of these boards.

Starter-like and reliever-like describe observed usage — batted balls per appearance — and nothing more. This data carries no roster role, so no pitcher here is identified as a starter, a reliever or a closer.

What these boards leave out

{{ below_minimum_count }} pitcher-seasons with fewer than {{ pitchers.board_display_minimum_bbe }} resolved batted balls are not ranked above. Each still has its own page, with its total, its rate and its interval shown in full. Below that many batted balls a per-100 rate carries an interval wide enough to reach both ends of this population — a single batted ball can put a pitcher at the very top or the very bottom of it — so a ranked position would read as a finding it cannot support. That is a choice about this board, not a requirement any of those seasons failed.
